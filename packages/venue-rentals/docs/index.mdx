## Why this exists

A venue can rent things out with a pod booking: a racket, court shoes, a projector. The guest pays
for them through Duncit with the seat, Duncit keeps its rental commission and pays the rest to the
venue. Three consoles look at the same rentals:

- **Finance** sees every rental, records the refunds it pays back, and sets the rules (Finance >
  Rented Items > Settings).
- **The venue owner** (Partners console) marks each item returned or damaged and sees its numbers.
- **The Venues console** does the same for any venue its editors look after.

So the board, the dialogs and the tiles are one package instead of three copies. The native app
cannot import MUI, so everything that is not drawing lives in **`@duncit/utils` →
`venue-rentals.ts`**: the GraphQL documents as plain strings, the row types, the label keys
(`rentalStatusKey`, `rentalRefundKey`, `rentalUnitKey`), the rules (`canMarkRental`,
`canRecordRentalRefund`, `rentalMaxQuantity`) and the client twin of the server's price
(`rentalLinePrice` for one item's rent and deposit, the breakdown Pod Rent prints under each
picked item; `rentalSelectionTotals` sums those lines). Copy is the `venueRentals` namespace.

## How the money moves

1. **Checkout.** The guest picks items with their seat. The server prices them (rate × hours for an
   HOUR item, × 1 for a SLOT item, deposit per unit). Coupons and coins apply to the ticket only.
2. **Capture.** Inside the payment's transaction each line reserves the pod's stock atomically. A line
   that sold out since checkout is recorded **UNAVAILABLE** with its money due back; the seat still books.
3. **Outcome.** Items are handed over AT THE VENUE and their deposits are settled there. The venue
   checks the returned item and marks **RETURNED** (it hands the whole deposit back in cash) or
   **DAMAGED** (it hands back the deposit less its claim). Duncit never refunds such a deposit
   through the gateway: it pays the WHOLE deposit to the venue in its payout, so the venue is made
   whole for the cash it handed back. `deposit_at_venue` records that cash (0 when Finance made
   deposits non-refundable). An item never marked is closed as returned after the configured days. A
   guest whose backout spot is filled is **RELEASED** (the item never went out, so Duncit refunds the
   deposit, and the rent per the settings). A cancelled pod returns the rentals with the payment's own
   refund (**IN_PAYMENT**).
4. **Payout.** An hourly sweep raises one **VENUE_RENTAL** payout per ended pod from the settled bookings
   (rent less commission, plus the deposits settled at the venue); its notes split the two.
   Finance approves it in Payment Release like any other.

Every outcome freezes `refund_due`, `deposit_at_venue`, `venue_amount` and the commission on the
booking, so a later settings change never rewrites a closed rental. `rentalHandBack` in
`@duncit/utils` is the client twin the mark dialogs use to say "hand ₹X back now".

## Categories

Every item names one or more Super → Category → Sub rows (`RentalItemCategories`, the shared
`@duncit/category` rows field). A pod offers the item only when its club's Super + Sub is one of
them; the server enforces it in the catalogue, in Pod Rent's pod list and at checkout. An item with
no rows is offered to no pod.

## The desk

`VenueRentalDesk` is the whole venue side on one page, with URL-selected tabs: **Dashboard** (the
numbers and the bookings), **Items** (the rented-items editor: name, photos, per slot or per hour,
rate, deposit, units per pod, most per guest, available) and **Bookings**. The editor saves through
`updateApprovedVenue`, so only an approved venue lists items, and every kept item sends its id so its
bookings stay linked.

## The venue's own rental terms

Duncit's rental terms come from Finance > Rented Items > Settings. A venue can add its own beside
them: the Items tab has a rich-text **Rental terms** box (`RentalTermsField`, `@duncit/rich-text`),
written by the venue owner (Partners console) or any Venues-console editor and saved with the items
as `rental_terms_html` (at most `rental_terms_max` characters). The pod's rental catalogue returns it
as `venue_terms_html`, and checkout shows **both** sections — Duncit's first, then the venue's —
sanitised before rendering. A venue that stops offering rentals has its terms hidden from guests.

## GST on deposits — per venue

Whether a deposit is charged GST is a venue decision, made only in the **Venues console**:
`VenueRentalItems` in `mode="admin"` shows a switch (`RentalDepositGstSwitch`) that calls
`setVenueRentalDepositGst` (VENUE_EDIT role, audited on the venue). Off keeps the deposit in the bill
but outside the taxable value — the rent is always taxed. The choice in force is frozen into the
payment's metadata, so flipping it never re-prices a payment already made. The owner never sees the
switch.

## On the app

The native app has the same desk (`VenueRentedItems` screen, `/venues/rented-items`), drawn in
Tamagui on the shared pieces: the same GraphQL documents, `makeRentalItemsSchema` /
`makeMarkRentalSchema` from `@duncit/forms/schemas`, and the same label maps. The app reads the
venue's rental terms; it links to the Partners console to edit them, where the rich-text editor is.

## Usage

```tsx
import { FinanceRentalBookings, VenueRentalBookings, VenueRentalStatsTiles } from '@duncit/venue-rentals';

// Finance > Rented Items
<FinanceRentalBookings />

// A venue's desk: the host page picks the venue.
<VenueRentalDesk venueId={venueId} surface="partners-app" mode="owner" />

// Or its parts:
<VenueRentalStatsTiles venueId={venueId} />
<VenueRentalBookings venueId={venueId} surface="partners-app" />
```

`surface` prefixes the table id, so each console remembers its own column choices.
